ui-science — verified primary sources

Every rule in rules.json cites one of these. Each was verified at its primary venue this session (fetched, not asserted). Verdicts: EVIDENCE (someone measured a human) · OPINION (influential assertion) · FOLKLORE (debunked / misapplied).

The tell: real UI science bottoms out in a measurement of a human — a reaction time, a color-discrimination ellipse, a recall rate, an eye-track.

Tier 1 — hard, measured, formula-grade

EVIDENCE — Fitts 1954

Full
Fitts, P.M. "The information capacity of the human motor system in controlling the amplitude of movement." J. Experimental Psychology 47(6):381 (1954).
Formula
MT = a + b·log₂(2D/W) — movement time as a function of distance D and target width W.
Use
Targets as large/close as feasible; screen-edge targets are effectively infinite-depth. 44px/48dp are platform guidelines built around this, not measured constants.
Rule
motor.fitts-target-size

EVIDENCE — MacAdam 1942 → OKLCH

Full
MacAdam, D.L. "Visual Sensitivities to Color Differences in Daylight." J. Optical Society of America 32(5):247 (1942).
Finding
25,000 color-match trials → color space is perceptually NON-uniform (MacAdam ellipses); CIE 1931 xy cannot be made uniform by any projective transform.
Chain
This is why HSL lies (equal HSL steps ≠ equal perceived steps) and why OKLab/OKLCH (Ottosson 2020) exist — engineered so equal numeric steps ≈ equal perceived steps.
Rule
color.oklch-ramp-uniformity

STANDARD — WCAG 2.1 contrast

Full
W3C WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 1.4.3 (Contrast Minimum), W3C Recommendation (2018).
Caveat
Relative-luminance ratio is the enforceable standard; APCA / WCAG 3 (draft) model perceived contrast better in some cases. Ratio is what ships today.
Rule
color.wcag-contrast-aa

Tier 2 — real but conditional

EVIDENCE — Cowan 2001 (supersedes Miller)

Full
Cowan, N. "The magical number 4 in short-term memory: a reconsideration of mental storage capacity." Behavioral & Brain Sciences 24(1):87 (2001).
Finding
Working-memory capacity ≈ 4 chunks, not 7. Miller 1956's "7±2" was serial recall of unidimensional stimuli, not UI chunking — Miller himself called it near-coincidence.
Rule
memory.no-cross-step-recall (and folklore flag folklore.magical-seven)

EVIDENCE — Cleveland & McGill 1984

Full
Cleveland, W.S. & McGill, R. "Graphical Perception: Theory, Experimentation, and Application to the Development of Graphical Methods." J. American Statistical Association 79(387):531 (1984).
Finding
Ranked accuracy of decoding: position > length > angle > area > color. Replicated on Mechanical Turk by Heer & Bostock (CHI 2010).
Rule
dataviz.encoding-rank

EVIDENCE — Weissgerber 2015

Full
Weissgerber, T.L. et al. "Beyond Bar and Line Graphs: Time for a New Data Presentation Paradigm." PLOS Biology 13(4):e1002128 (2015).
Finding
Review of 703 papers: "many different data distributions produce the identical bar graph." Bars of means hide the data; show the distribution for small n.
Rule
dataviz.bar-hides-distribution

Tier 4 — folklore & overstated claims (flagged when invoked)

FOLKLORE — "max 7 items (Miller)"

Wrong number (Cowan: ~4) AND wrong domain (serial recall ≠ visual layout). No scientific "7 widgets" cap. Flag: folklore.magical-seven.

FOLKLORE — "design for the F-pattern"

Nielsen Norman Group, "F-Shaped Pattern of Reading on the Web" (eye-tracking, ~45–47 users/example, 2006). NN/g's own finding: the F-pattern is the DEFAULT when cues are absent, is "bad for users," and should be COUNTERACTED with hierarchy — not emulated. Flag: folklore.design-for-f-pattern.

OVERSTATED — "always maximize data-ink (Tufte)"

Bateman et al., "Useful Junk? The Effects of Visual Embellishment on Comprehension and Memorability of Charts." ACM CHI 2010, pp.2573–2582. Embellished charts were no worse for comprehension and BETTER for long-term recall — refuting the strong form of Tufte's data-ink orthodoxy. Treat data-ink as taste, not law. Flag: folklore.maximize-data-ink.
